- Cars and motos check the strip along their own path, at their own width, against the bus body and the articulated trailer. The old check looked in a straight line from the car's nose and ignored the trailer, so cars drove into a trailer swinging across their lane.
- A moving bus counts as already where it will be in 1.5 s, and a reversing one as where it will be in 3 s plus 3 m. Traffic then brakes for a bus pulling out or cutting in before it is in the way, and stops well short of a bus backing up.
- A bus is only waited for at a junction if it is in the part the car is about to cross. One driving away ahead is followed, but one coming round a roundabout towards the entry is given way to.

- A pass ends only when nothing is beside the vehicle or within 20 m ahead in its usual line, so it never cuts back in on the bus and never swings in between two cones.
- Motos keep filtering past cars, but only where they do not overlap them, and at most 4 m/s faster than the car beside them. A car in their line, such as one going round the bus, is followed like any other.
- Motos squeeze past a stopped bus or a prop without waiting when they stay within half a metre of the centre line, at 4 m/s beside the bus. Further out they wait for a gap like a car.
- Props in the road (cones, barriers, bins, stalls, parked or shoved cars) are filed under the lanes they are beside as spans along and across the lane. Checking them is then a few sums per vehicle, and the list is rebuilt only when a prop moves.
- A knocked-over cone is driven over. A bin, crate or barrier lying in the road is still gone round.

- A car kept waiting 7 to 9 s behind the bus gives one short toot. It is the bus horn played higher and for a quarter of a second, so no new sound was added.
- Vehicles read the ground height every fourth tick and follow the slope in between. That cut the drawing cost by about a third, and nobody can see the difference.

- A vehicle going round the bus that is still beside its back half stops once the doors shut, and lets the bus pull away, instead of racing it.
- Where the lane moves over for parked cars or cones, the moto's kerb line moves over by half as much. Motos then ease round them; before, they stopped dead at a parked car just past a junction and a turning bus clipped them.

- Being over the free speed only ever brakes at the comfortable 2.6 m/s². Only something in the way brakes harder, up to 7 m/s². Before, a speed cap such as the moto filtering limit braked at over 100 m/s².

- Downpours are `weather.rain` in pack.json, read by `RainSettings` with a default for anything missing, and checked by `CountryPack.validate()`.
- `WeatherPlan` draws every number from the shift seed before deciding whether it rains, so forcing rain for a test or the autopilot keeps the same timing a planned downpour on that seed would have.
- The downpour starts 20 to 50% of the way through the timetable, so it always lands mid-shift whatever the route's length.
- An aguacero has three parts: a 15 to 25 s build-up where the clouds lead and the rain grows as a cube (a few first drops, then a rush), 110 to 190 s of rain with slow gusts, and a 20 to 30 s clearing. Chances: dawn 10%, morning 15%, midday 30%, sunset 50%, night 25%, so the afternoon is the likeliest.
- Road wetness lags the rain: it soaks in about 6 s and dries over 90 s after the rain stops, so the road stays dark, shiny and slippery for a while after the sun is back.
- Wet grip scales the brakes and the lateral grip limit, down to 0.7 when soaked. Full braking from 50 km/h takes 21.8 m dry and 29.2 m soaked, and the steering limit at 40 km/h drops from 6.0 to 4.2 degrees: noticeable, but a driver who brakes early is fine.
- Pulling away is not limited by grip. A bus rarely spins its wheels, and a slower start would only feel laggy.
- Traffic on a soaked road drives at 0.8 of its speed and keeps 1.6 times its time gap (the standing gap grows by half that), through a `gap_scale` passed to the Intelligent Driver Model.
- The timetable clock runs 20% slower on a soaked road (`RouteSystem.rain_slack`), so a careful driver is never made late by a downpour. The report still shows the real time taken.
- Downpour bonus: no collision, knock or emergency stop from when it pours hard (rain 0.35) until it is nearly over (rain 0.1) earns 60 coins and 2 points of passenger satisfaction, with a line in the report. A rough downpour costs nothing extra. It is small so rain is a chance to shine, not a requirement.

- The shift report carries `time_of_day` and `downpour`, and its counts `downpours` and `clean_downpours`, ready for daily challenges.
- Night is faked, not lit: windows glow from the facade shader, lamps, stall bulbs and shelter strips are unshaded materials whose colour is turned up after dark, and the pools of light are additive discs. No real lights in the city.
- Lit windows are chosen in the facade shader from the window's place and the building's vertex alpha, its own share of lit windows. That costs no geometry and no draws. The share comes from a separate random stream (seed 2207), so the town's layout (seed 1187) is unchanged.
- Walls moved from StandardMaterial3D to a small spatial shader. In daylight it draws the same texture, tint and roughness; after dark it only adds emission in lit panes. Shops light most of their windows (open late), offices fewest.
- Lamp and stall pools are one MultiMesh in ObstacleField, tilted to the slope under each, so a knocked lamp or stall loses its pool. Shelter pools are one static mesh that follows the ground.
- Bus lights: glowing head, tail and cabin strip on every level; a SpotLight3D headlight beam on MEDIUM and HIGH; a cabin OmniLight3D on HIGH only, whose cull mask reaches only render layer 2, which every bus mesh joins, so it costs nothing outside the bus.
- Traffic lamps are a second surface on the car and moto batches with one shader: lamps ahead of the middle are white and those behind red, so one extra draw per batch covers both and ignores the car's paint colour.
- Sun shadows are off at night (data) and fade out as clouds thicken, switching off at 60% cloud. A night or a downpour therefore draws less than a sunny day.
- Headlights (bus and traffic) come on at night and when the clouds pass 0.4; street lamps come on to half under a full storm and windows to 35% of the lamp light.
- Rain is a shader on one (LOW) or two (MEDIUM, HIGH) open cylinders that follow the camera, with streaks worked out from position. No particles and no texture.
- Raindrops on the windscreen are a quad with a shader that works out when a blade last passed each spot from the wipers' cosine sweep, so drops grow back behind the blades with no render target. Off on LOW. The wipers run above rain 0.04, from a 2.0 s sweep in light rain to 1.1 s in a downpour, and finish their sweep to rest.
- The rain, windscreen and pool materials are drawn unseen for the first frames of a shift, so their shaders are built at the start rather than with a stutter when the first drops fall mid-shift.
